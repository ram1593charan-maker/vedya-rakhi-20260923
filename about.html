Our Story

A mother's love, woven into every thread.

Where it started

It began with a single rakhi in 1996.

My mother made a rakhi for my brother Aditya when he was five years old. She didn't have money for a "real" rakhi from the shop — so she sat at the kitchen table with red thread, a few gold beads from her wedding jewelry box, and a borrowed needle.

It took her almost the whole afternoon. When Aditya saw it, he wore it for three days straight, even to school. That single handmade rakhi became a tradition — every Raksha Bandhan, every Bhai Dooj, she made him one.

Twenty-nine years later, she's still at that same table. Now there are nine of us siblings. And every August, we line up like we did when we were children, waiting for our number.

Why we sell

We started selling because the cousins asked.

A few years ago, my cousins saw the rakhis she made for us and asked if she'd make some for them too. Then their friends. Then friends of friends.

What started as ten orders in a single August became 500+ pieces in just a few seasons. We don't advertise. We don't run ads. Every order comes through WhatsApp, through someone who knew someone who wore one of her rakhis once and never forgot.

This is not a business. It's a kitchen-table operation run by a 65-year-old woman with very steady hands and three hours of patience for every single piece.

The craft

Three hours per piece. Zero shortcuts.

Each rakhi starts with handpicked silk thread — never polyester. The beads are glass, brass, or pearl — sourced from a single shop in Pune we've been buying from for 25 years. No plastic. No shortcuts. No machines.

She knots each piece the way her own mother taught her. She uses no patterns, no templates — every rakhi is slightly different from every other, even when they're "the same design." That's the point.

When she's done, she wraps each one in a small hand-stamped box with a tiny card. Then she ships it from the post office herself, twice a week.
